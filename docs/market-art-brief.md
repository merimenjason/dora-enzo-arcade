# Night Market · art brief

Paintings and cut-outs for Night Market (`/market`), a lantern-lit market stall game with haggling. Over one moon month Dora and Enzo run a little stall at the riverside Night Market: they buy wares at dusk, sell them by lantern light, read their customers and save up for a festival lantern boat. The game draws the stall, the wares, the lanterns, the customers' tells, the paper texture and its own woodblock stand-in for every painting until the file loads, so the art can arrive in any order. Written 01-11-2026.

**A new art style for this game: Japanese woodblock print (ukiyo-e and shin-hanga).** Flat black ink keylines and a limited set of flat colour blocks (indigo, Prussian blue, vermilion, ochre, soft moss green, plum and cream), visible wood grain in the colour blocks and washi paper fibre, gentle bokashi gradients in the night sky and the river, and lantern glow as soft colour bleeds. No text, letters, kanji, signatures or seal stamps in any image: the game adds its own red seals in the UI.

**Dora and Enzo (critical).** They read instantly as chinchillas: a plump round body, a big round head with a broad, slightly flat, smooth crown with a soft short-fur edge, a short blunt muzzle, large round pink ears on the sides of the crown with nothing else on the head, a big bushy tail and short whiskers. **Dora** is small and cream-white with ruby-red eyes. **Enzo** is a little bigger and cool blue-grey (never taupe, beige or brown) with a pale grey chest and solid black eyes. No clothes. Compare `public/art/driftwood/title.webp` and `public/art/roll/title.webp` for the head and body shape; never the Hayloft Tycoon portraits.

**Facing.** Dora and Enzo stand behind the stall on the left of the night scene, so every cut-out of them faces RIGHT, toward the customer. Every customer stands in front of the stall on the right, so every customer cut-out faces LEFT, toward the stall.

All files live in `public/art/market/` as `.webp`. Cut-outs are painted on flat #00FF00 and keyed with Pillow (soft edge, green despill), cropped and set on a transparent square.

The customers' tells (ears pricked or flat, a tail flick, the lean of their posture, a long look at a ware, the weight of their purse, a return visit) are drawn by the game over the cut-outs as ink marks, so the paintings stay simple and calm.
