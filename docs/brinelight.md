# Brinelight

Game 98, at `/brinelight`. A two-hero tag-team belt brawler on a harbour at festival night. Pinchwell's crab gang has
snuffed the lanterns of Brinelight; Dora and Enzo win them back fight by fight, and every string of lanterns they
relight warms the night wash off the town. Areas 1 (Lantern Quay), 2 (The Fish Market) and 3 (The Ropewalk and
Shipyard) are built; areas 4 to 6 are on the map and in the save format but not built yet (see
[What is left](#what-is-left)).

Written 12-11-2026. The pitch and the feel test came first (`docs/codex-magnum-opus.md`, round two); this is the full
build of the first three districts. Area 3 was added the same day ([The Ropewalk and Shipyard](#the-ropewalk-and-shipyard)).

## Code layout

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `lib/brinelight-levels.ts` | The hand-authored content: six areas, nine stages, twenty-one scenes and three hidden ones, laid out as string rows (a decor row behind the railing, five walkable lanes, a decor row in front), with each scene's waves, bosses, talks, lantern strings, secrets and belt. Nothing in it is random. |
| `lib/brinelight-game.ts` | The engine: fixed 60 Hz step, seeded randomness (a seed replays a run exactly), no DOM. Heroes, foes, props, items, bosses, the camera lock, prompts, the Festival meter, the save helpers. Sounds and effects leave it as `events`. |
| `lib/brinelight-scene.ts` | Drawing only, Canvas 2D: parallax far painting, floor strips, lantern strings and the night wash, depth-sorted sprites, effects, the HUD, the prompts, the touch controls and the portrait layout. Reads the engine, never changes it. |
| `lib/brinelight-art.ts` | Generated by `scripts/brinelight-art.py`: every frame's place in the seven atlases and its anchor. |
| `app/brinelight/page.tsx` | Title, harbour map, story panels, the stage, pause and results; keys, touch and gamepads; the loop; the save. |
| `app/brinelight/sound.ts` | WebAudio: every sound effect and five small tunes, all synthesised. |
| `public/art/brinelight/` | Seven WebP atlases (dora, enzo, foes, bosses, ui, props, area3), seven floor strips, seventeen far paintings, the title, the map, four story panels and the medal: about 11 MB in all. |

`window.__brinelight()` returns `{ game, scene, screen, paused, save, mode, labels, start(stage, seed?) }` for the
browser tests, and `main[data-ready="true"]` is set once the art has loaded.

## Controls

| | Keys | Touch | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|---|
| Move (across and in depth) | arrows or WASD | the stick | left stick or d-pad |
| Attack | J or X | ATTACK | X (or B) |
| Grab (Enzo) | walk into a dazed foe, a shelled hermit, a shell or a barrel without pressing Attack | push the stick into it | the same |
| Throw | Attack while holding | ATTACK | X |
| Jump | K, Z or Space | JUMP | A |
| Tag | L, C or Shift | TAG | Y or a shoulder |
| Dash | tap left or right twice | flick the stick twice | double-tap the stick or d-pad |
| Dash attack | Attack during a dash | the same | the same |
| Special | dash, then Jump during or just after it, then Attack in the air | flick, flick, JUMP, ATTACK | the same |
| Double-team | Attack + Jump together with the Festival meter full | ATTACK + JUMP | X + A |
| Pause | Esc or P | the pause button | Start |

Prompts are drawn in the player's own keys: the key last used for each action, or the touch or gamepad name once
those are in use. On a phone held upright (390×844) the play view takes about three quarters of the screen, zoomed to
about 440 world units across. It follows the hero and frames the nearest threat, pulling back to as much as 700 units
when a boss or foe stands too far off, with arrows at the edges for foes out of sight. The controls sit in a compact
panel under it (27% of the height, 190 to 300 px): the stick on the left, ATTACK, TAG and JUMP in a diamond on the
right, every button at least 56 px across (a key card instead when an upright window is played with keys or a pad). On its side (844×390) the controls float over the deck corners.

## The rules

**Two heroes, one on the boards.** Dora is quick and light: four kicks (the fourth a knock-down roundhouse), a diving
kick, and a rising kick that knocks diving gulls out of the air. Enzo is slow and heavy: two paws and a two-fisted slam
that ripples along the deck, an air hammer, and a grab. Attack always punches; Enzo grabs by **walking into** a dazed foe, a
shelled hermit, a puffed puffer, a lobster, a barrel or a loose shell, as long as Attack hasn't been pressed in the last
0.35 s, so a mashing Enzo keeps punching instead of carrying crabs about. One Attack throws what he holds. The first
time he stands near a dazed foe, a one-time prompt (his walk key, "walk in to grab") shows how. Boss moments (ripping
Clamps' barrel, pinning the Admiral, throwing Kid Knuckles) stay on Attack beside the dizzy boss, since those are
prompted windows. The other hero waits at the edge of the screen and
heals slowly; Tag swaps them, and the hero coming in strikes on arrival.

**The grey/cream rule.** Grey things want Enzo (shells, armour, barrels, the lobster's guard, a grounded admiral);
cream things want Dora (wings, anything in the air). The wrong hero's hit goes *tink*: a pale burst, the right hero's
face over the foe for a moment, and after two wrong hits in a row the partner's portrait glows and a prompt says whose
job it is. Glints on foes say the same thing before you hit them.

**Tag chains.** Tagging in during a combo starts a chain: +15% damage per level (up to three), a burst of Festival
meter and score. The Festival meter fills with hits and chains; when it is full, Attack + Jump together calls the
other hero in for the double-team, which knocks down everything on screen.

**The foes.** Crabs (area 1's mashing fodder), hermits (shelled: Dora tinks; Enzo's grab pops the shell off, and the
shell becomes something to throw or kick), gulls (fly; Dora's), puffers (puff up into spines that hurt a kicking Dora;
Enzo can grab one puffed), shrimp lookouts (whistle up more foes if you don't reach them in three seconds), lobsters
(block from the front, counter on the third blocked hit; go round the back, or let Enzo break the guard), jellies
(charge and zap), and urchins (spines hurt anyone hitting them from the ground, until a slam, an air attack or the
special flips them). Heavy foes have armour during their committed attacks. Knocked-down foes can't be hit until they
are up again. At most one or two foes attack at once.

**Area 1 stays easy for a casual masher.** 1-1's first scene lets one foe attack at a time; the tutorial teaches move,
attack, the hermit (tag Enzo in) and the gull (jump-kick with Dora) by prompts, not text. Area 2 mixes the foes that
punish mashing (lobsters, urchins, puffers, jellies, shrimp), hits 25% harder with assist on and 60% harder with it
off, and lets three foes attack at once with assist off. With assist on, the hits that punish mashing (a lobster's
counter, puffer and urchin spines, a charged jelly's zap) grow with every wrong hit in a row: +150% per wrong hit after
the first, up to eight times (`PUNISH` and `PUNISH_CAP` in the engine). Once either hero has been knocked out, or after
a retry, it eases off for the rest of the stage, so nobody gets stuck. A player who reads the foes, or tags when it
tinks, hardly notices; a pure masher who ignores the tink and the prompt sometimes needs one retry.

**Assist** is on by default: a wider dash window, more reach for the grab, softer hits (60%), faster healing while
waiting, longer boss windows.

### The Ropewalk and Shipyard

Area 3 is a step up: with assist on, hits land 60% harder than in area 1 (area 2: 25%), 75% harder with assist off;
foes have 30% more health and attack 28% sooner. The mash punishments and the three-attackers rule carry on from area 2.

**The belt.** In all eight area-3 scenes (not the hidden sail loft), tar barrels roll down the ropewalk belt while a
fight is on, all through Tanglejaw's fight and in Longshanks' third phase: one at a time, every 8 to 11 s, down the lanes the scene names
(`belt` in the scene). Each one rumbles first and runs a line of chevrons along its lane from the right edge for
1.7 s (1.35 s with assist off), then rolls through. Step into another lane or jump it; standing in its way knocks you
down for 11 (12 with assist off, before the area's scaling), and it bowls foes over too. Enzo's Attack stops a rolling
barrel dead ("STOPPED!"), and it is his to pick up and throw; Dora's kick just tinks. Enzo also picks a barrel up by
walking into it up or down the deck, not only across. Hal, the ropewalk's rigger, says all this as you meet it, and
the first barrels bring a move prompt (and, for Enzo, an Attack prompt).

**New foes, by the grey/cream rule.**
- **Eels** live in holes in the planks. One peeks, blows bubbles for 0.85 s, then lunges along its lane (170 units)
  and lies stretched out for a moment, dazed, where anyone can hit it and Enzo can grab it. While it only peeks,
  Dora's kicks make it duck (and tink: it is Enzo's); Enzo's paws haul it out onto the boards, where it fights like
  any foe. Left alone for 4.5 s it moves to a hole in your lane.
- **Cormorants** circle up high. A ring follows you on the boards, then locks, and the bird dives straight down onto
  it; a missed dive sticks its beak in the planks for 2.4 s (1.7 s), dazed for Enzo to grab. In the air it is
  Dora's: her rising kick knocks it out of the dive.
- **Inkers** (small octopuses) drop onto a post at the back, puff, and squirt ink across the screen: up to three
  blots that fade after 3.5 s (5 s), and the jet stings for 6. Perched, an inker is Dora's: Enzo's paws can't reach
  it, though the slam at the end of his combo or a thrown barrel knocks it off. Knocked off its post, its ink clears
  and it fights on the ground.
- Remixed from earlier areas: lined-up crabs (each stage has a wave that walks in single file for the special),
  hermits, gulls, urchins, lobsters, shrimp lookouts and puffers alongside the new three.

Tar barrels with a lantern charm sealed inside (`q` in a lane row) glint; break one open, or throw it, to free the charm.

### The dash and the special

- **Dash:** tap left or right twice. The first tap must be short (at most 0.25 s) and the second must come within
  0.25 s of letting go with assist on, 0.2 s with it off. Dora dashes at 760 u/s for 0.2 s, Enzo at 600 u/s for
  0.24 s; it stops dead, with no slide. A double tap also cancels the recovery of a ground attack, so dashing in and
  out of a combo works.
- **Dash attack:** Attack during a dash. Dora's flying knee and Enzo's shoulder barge both knock down.
- **The special:** dash, press Jump during the dash or within 0.15 s after it (the jump keeps the dash's speed), then
  Attack in the air.
  - Dora's **Comet Spin** is a spinning flying kick that travels through the lane at 640 u/s for up to 0.48 s,
    knocking down everything in it.
  - Enzo's **Anchor Drop** is a high arc into a diving body-press; the landing sends a shockwave 200 units round.
  - Both have a hit pause, screen shake, painted take-off, flight and impact frames and their own sound. Landing has
    a short recovery (Dora 0.32 s, Enzo 0.4 s) and a 0.5 s cooldown. On a boss the special does well under a tenth of its health: the boss's own rules
    decide the damage, so it can't skip a pattern.
- **Teaching it:** the first time three or more foes stand in a line ahead in one lane, a one-time prompt shows the
  move in the player's own keys ("→→ then K then J"; on touch "flick →→, JUMP, ATTACK"; on a pad "▶▶ then A then
  X"). Each area has a wave of crabs that walks in lined up in one lane (q3 and s1 in area 1, m3 and sq1 in area 2)
  so the prompt appears in play; if the player stands in another lane, the prompt first says to line up.

### The bosses

Every boss needs both heroes; none is a grind (casual bots take 25 to 60 s with assist on).

| Boss | Where | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Crusty Magnus** (mid-boss) | 1-2, Bell Steps | Wears barnacle armour: Enzo's heavy hits break it piece by piece (Dora tinks). Bare, he is Dora's to juggle. Regrows armour if left alone. |
| **Clamps the Dockboss** | 1-3, his pier | Lives in a barrel: rocks, then rolls across the deck (chevrons run along the lane he will roll down). Dizzy after a roll, Enzo rips the barrel off; bare, Dora keeps him in the air. Phase 3 wears two barrels. |
| **Kid Knuckles** (mid-boss) | 2-2, Fishmongers' Ring | A mantis-shrimp boxer. His tell is the counter window for Dora; his guard needs an Enzo throw; his hop is knocked down by Dora. |
| **The Gull Admiral** | 2-3, the awnings | Shrieks and dives: Dora counters a dive into a tumble and grounds him; Enzo pins him for the damage. Swoops in phase 2, drops nets in phase 3. |
| **Longshanks the Rigger** (mid-boss) | 3-2, Timber Stacks | A spider crab on rigging stilts. Up on them, hits from the ground do 8%; Dora's jump kicks reach his body at 90%, Enzo's air hammer at 60%. His stomp: a ring follows you, locks, and the leg comes down and sticks in the planks for 3.2 s (2.4 s); Enzo's Attack beside the stuck leg heaves him over, and a thrown barrel or a belt barrel bowls him over too. Toppled for 5 s (3.8 s), he takes Dora's kicks at 2.4×. Phase 2 adds a leg sweep (a wide ring; jump it); phase 3 starts the belt and calls a cormorant. |
| **Old Tanglejaw** | 3-3, the drydock | A great moray under the drydock with three drain holes. He blows bubbles at one, then strikes out along its lane (chevrons mark it; up to 300 units, 340 with assist off; out of the end holes he always strikes towards the middle). With fewer than two holes plugged he slips straight back and hits do 15%. Enzo carries barrels (two wait on the boards and the drydock belt sends more, which stop and stay) onto the holes: walk onto one or press Attack beside it; plugging the hole he is bubbling in bonks him. With two plugged he must use the last hole, and after a strike he lies stuck for 4.2 s (3 s), taking Dora's kicks at 3×. Each new phase opens with a roar that blows the barrels out again; phase 2 adds a tail whip (a ring; jump it) and calls eels, phase 3 a cormorant. |

**How bosses teach, without words.**
- Every dangerous move marks the boards first: a dashed, pulsing ring where a stomp, swipe, punch, hop or dive will
  land (Crusty Magnus' stomp, Clamps' swipe, Kid Knuckles' punch and hop, the Admiral's dive, a falling net), and
  marching chevrons along the lane a roll or swoop will sweep (Clamps' roll, the Admiral's wing swoop).
- The first time each telegraph shows, the boss holds the pose half as long again (`b.first`, 1.5×), so the wind-up
  is easy to read once; after that it runs at normal speed.
- At the start of each phase, once, a hint icon floats over the boss for about three and a half seconds: the face of
  the hero whose job the phase is, and the key to press (that hero's Attack or Jump, or Tag when they are waiting at
  the edge). Brute: Enzo, then Dora, then Enzo. Clamps: Enzo, then Dora's jump, then Enzo. Kid Knuckles: Dora, then
  Enzo, then Dora's jump. The Admiral: Dora's jump, then Enzo, then Dora's jump. Longshanks: Enzo, then Dora's jump,
  then Enzo. Tanglejaw: Enzo carrying (the hint reads "barrels → holes"), then Dora's jump, then Enzo carrying.
- In area 3 the same marks cover the foes and the belt: chevrons along a belt barrel's lane, two chevrons ahead of an
  eel's lunge, a ring under a cormorant's dive, Longshanks' stomp and sweep rings, Tanglejaw's lane chevrons and whip
  ring. The tag prompt names the job ("Enzo's job: barrels into the holes", "Dora's job: kick him while he's down").

The wrong hero tinks and gets the tag prompt. A retry after both heroes go down restores the boss by 15% and gives the
heroes 70 health.

## The save

`localStorage['brinelight-v1']`: `{ v: 1, assist, cleared: { [stage]: { score, time, medal } }, found: [secret ids],
seen: [story panels], sound, music, lanterns }`. Stages open in order (`stageOpen`), so 3-1 opens once 2-3 is
cleared; areas 4 to 6 have no stages yet, so the map shows them locked. Area 3 needed no change to the save. A new area only needs its stages added to `STAGES` and its ids to `AREAS[n].stages`.
Change the version if the shape changes.

## The art

Everything visible is a Codex painting; code draws only effects (sparks, dust, splashes, rings, glows, shadows, the
night wash, telegraph rings) and HUD text.

- [`docs/brinelight-art/style.md`](brinelight-art/style.md) is the palette and style guide, written first.
- [`docs/brinelight-art/jobs.txt`](brinelight-art/jobs.txt) holds the 65 prompts; [`gen.sh`](brinelight-art/gen.sh)
  runs one with `codex exec`, passing the two mock-ups (`docs/ideas/brinelight-play.webp`, `brinelight-boss.webp`,
  as PNG) and, for the heroes, `docs/bellspire-art/ref-pair.png` and the first hero sheet as references.
- **Far paintings:** one or two per district, so neighbouring scenes don't share a skyline: the harbour with its
  lighthouse (q1, and mirrored for q3), the net sheds and chapel further along (q2, job `far2`), the harbour mouth by
  moonlight from the breakwater (bw, `far3`); the café street (s1) and the customs house and dome steps (s2,
  `townfar2`); the market hall's arches (m1, m5, mirrored for m3) and its ice-house corner (m2, mirrored for m4,
  `marketfar2`); the clock-tower square (sq1) and the fountain side with ship masts (sq2, `squarefar2`); the ropewalk
  shed (r1, mirrored for r3, `ropewalkfar`) and its tar-boiling yard (r2, `ropewalkfar2`); the slipway with a hull on
  the stocks (y1, mirrored for y3, `shipyardfar`) and the scaffold side (y2, `shipyardfar2`); the sail loft
  (`loftfar`); the drydock's stone basin (d2, `drydockfar`) and its steps (d1, `drydockfar2`). The variants
  were painted with the original as a `-i` reference. A scene's painting is drawn wide enough that one scene never
  shows it twice; `farFlip` mirrors it.
- Sheets are painted on flat magenta, or green for violet things (the jelly, the urchin, Kid Knuckles).
  `python3 scripts/brinelight-art.py <folder>` keys the ground out and pulls the magenta cast out of grey fur and
  white feathers, cuts each grid, keeps the biggest blob per cell, scales each sheet by its idle frame (later hero
  sheets repeat the idle stance as frame 1 so they match), anchors heroes by the head and everything else by the
  centre of mass, packs six atlases and writes `lib/brinelight-art.ts`. The source PNGs stay outside the repo.
- Area 3 added 18 jobs: three floor strips, seven far paintings, the shipyard story panel, sheets for the eel, the
  cormorant, the inker (on green), Longshanks and Tanglejaw (each with its hole, post or stuck leg painted in), a
  props sheet (tar barrel, bobbin, scaffold, rope coil, planks, pulley, anchor, sawhorse, paint pots, figurehead,
  winch, lamp post) and Hal with his face. They pack into a seventh atlas, `atlas-area3.webp`. No new hero poses were
  needed: carrying and plugging use the existing hold, heave and throw frames.
- 318 frames. Each hero has 34 (idle ×2, walk ×4, dash, dash attack, three combo hits with wind-ups and a
  follow-through, jump, fall, land, two air attacks, hurt ×2, down, get-up, tag-in, pick-up, victory ×2, the special's
  take-off, two flight frames and impact, a face). Foes have 6 to 13 each, bosses 12, plus four NPCs with faces.

## Sound

All synthesised (`app/brinelight/sound.ts`), through a light echo bus and a compressor: thumps for kicks and paws, a
bright tink for the wrong hero, splashes, lantern chimes, a rising whistle for the Comet Spin and a falling thud for
the Anchor Drop; in area 3 an eel's bubbles, an inker's squirt and the thunk of a barrel into a hole. Music: an
accordion jig in D for Lantern Quay, a tarantella in A minor for the Fish Market, a hauling work song in G
mixolydian for the Ropewalk and Shipyard (with a mallet on an anvil every other beat), a low boss march, and a slow
tune on the map. Sound and music toggle separately.

  and moods, and `AREAS` marks them `ready: false`. Each needs: its stages and scenes in
  `lib/brinelight-levels.ts`; a floor strip and a far painting (add jobs to `jobs.txt`, then the `strip()` and
  `plain()` lists in the packer); its new foes (an `FD` entry, the behaviour in `updFoe`, the poses in `foePose`, a
  sheet in the packer); its boss (`BossKind`, `spawnBoss`, an `upd<Boss>` function, `bossMult`, `bossPose`, a sheet);
  a tune in `sound.ts` (its `mood`); a story panel; the map spot already exists. The save needs no change. Area 3 is
  the worked example: its `tier()` step in the engine, its scene mechanic (`belt` in the scene, `updBelt`), its foe
  and boss branches, the `area3` atlas group in the packer, the `worksong` tune and `panel-shipyard`.

## What is weakest

The first review's five weak spots (an automatic grab, bosses taught only by prompts, an assist-on Fish Market that
never made a masher retry, one far painting per district, and a cramped portrait view) were fixed on 12-11-2026. What
is weakest now:

- **Untried by a person:** the sound, gamepads, and a real phone (the touch tests use Chrome's touch emulation).
- **A player who never tags** has it hard in the Fish Market: with assist on the soloist bot needs a retry in about
  half the runs of 2-2 and 2-3, and with assist off four to ten retries a stage. The phase hints and the tag prompt
  point the way, but nothing forces the lesson.
- **The phase-hint icon** can sit next to a tag prompt for a moment at a phase change; both say the same thing, but
  it is busier than it needs to be.
- **Area 3's mid-boss is quick.** Longshanks takes the bots 25 to 30 s with assist on, about the same as area 2's Kid
  Knuckles, because a belt barrel or a thrown one topples him as surely as the heave.
- **The masher barely retries in area 3 with assist on** (one run in 24 of 3-2 and 3-3, none in 3-1, against 4 to 8%
  in area 2), though it takes 161 to 209 damage a stage. Area 3's step up shows in the casual bot's damage (93 to 141,
  up from 87 to 95) and with assist off, where the masher needs a retry in 17 to 71% of runs. Making the assisted
  masher retry more would have cost the casual bot its clean runs first.
- **Ink** covers part of the screen on purpose, and on a phone held upright three blots can hide a lot of a small
  view. It fades in 3.5 s with assist on and clears when the inker is knocked off.
- **The walk-in grab** has to be discovered: a one-time prompt shows it the first time Enzo stands near a dazed foe,
  and the hermit in the tutorial points the same way, but a player who skips both may never pick anything up.
