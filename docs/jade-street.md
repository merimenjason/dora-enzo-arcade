# Jade Street

Game 105, `/jade-street`. A top-down open-city caper set on the night before the Lantern Festival in Singapore’s Chinatown (its real public street names, with invented people, shops and jobs). The Kaypoh Crows have stolen five lucky heirlooms, and Dora (cream-white fur, red eyes, jade bomber with a gold crane) or Enzo (grey fur, vermilion tracksuit top) wins them back over 25 story jobs, then follows the crows out across the island for ten more. It is original: the quarter, its people, the jobs, the heat system and the interface were all made for this game.

**Seventeenth pass (21-11-2026): Pulau Ubin and chapter ten.** After the Lights by the Lake (job m65) Pulau Ubin opens: the island off Changi, reached only by Pak Ubin's bumboat from the Changi Point jetty (Route me plans the crossing; no MRT, no taxis, no cars), with Ubin Town, its jetty, the Wei Tuo Fa Gong Temple and its wayang stage, Ketam and Pekan Quarries, House No. 1, Jejawi Tower and the Chek Jawa boardwalk, rental bikes and a tandem, a kampong safe house, three properties and wildlife townsfolk (junglefowl, hornbills, box turtles, egrets, a mousedeer). A new gang, the Tusker Boys (cartoon wild boars, harmless), root about the dirt tracks. Chapter ten, "Kampong Spirit", is ten jobs (m66 to m75) with a new step, `track` (follow a boar trail print by print), and three Hawker Tales (k24 to k26). The chapter ends on a hook (a black-sailed junk slipping away south), not an ending.

**Sixteenth pass (20-11-2026): ride jobs.** The fifteenth pass's four rides get four Hawker Tales built around them, each with a giver: Uncle Buaya's ice-cream chime lures the Atas Bats off their posts for a cone while you photograph their hideout (Two Scoops), Coach Kerbau's dragon boat races bak chang down the Singapore River in a time that only a boat with the partner drumming makes (Bak Chang by Boat), the tandem's back saddle tosses nasi lemak to four barbecue pits along East Coast Park (Picnic Post), and a Skyline Luge cart chases a mynah down Siloso Road (Down Siloso Road). Six properties get their own stand paintings, Jurong Lake gets a smooth painted bank, Katong's ground is calm, Route me can chain two rides, a new night's contracts are dealt one a frame, and chapter nine's crow over Jurong Lake falls for a drummed dragon boat.

**Where the districts are (19-11-2026).** The districts now stand where they are in Singapore: Orchard Road, Clarke Quay and Tiong Bahru west of Chinatown (north to south, Fort Canning Park between Orchard Road and Chinatown), Jurong at the west edge over parkland, a beach and the sea, and the rest east and south as before. New roads join them over land (Outram Road, Bukit Timah Road, Clemenceau Avenue up to Orchard Road, the Ayer Rajah Expressway from Jurong); the long bridges from Kampong Glam are gone. See **Where the districts are** below.

**Fourteenth pass (18-11-2026): the south shore.** After the Christmas Star (job m55) the city grows south over the water into Tiong Bahru (the art-deco estate, Tiong Bahru Market, the bird corner) and Clarke Quay (the painted godowns on the Singapore River, Read Bridge, the Slingshot) and, from job m61, Jurong (the Science Centre, Snow City, Jem and the Chinese Garden on Jurong Lake). Chapter nine, *Lights by the Lake* (jobs m56 to m65 and Hawker Tales k17 to k19), sets Dora and Enzo against Towkay Hoot, the rival towkay, who is buying up every Mid-Autumn lantern on the island, and ends in a chase round Jurong Lake with a fourth new ending. The old neon quarter of Chinatown is renamed Club Street so that Clarke Quay can be the riverside district. Art fixes: Katong Park and Changi Airport stations repainted, calmer ground in Katong and Kampong Glam, and the switch box's own key. See **Tiong Bahru, Clarke Quay and Jurong (fourteenth pass)** below.

**Twelfth pass (16-11-2026): over the Geylang River.** After the Island Lights (job m45) the city grows east again, into Orchard Road (its malls, the Christmas light-up, Emerald Hill's townhouses), Katong and Joo Chiat (Peranakan shophouses, 328 Katong Laksa, East Coast Park's cycling track, the lagoon hawker centre and the beach) and, from job m52, Changi (the airport, the control tower, Changi Village and Jewel with its Rain Vortex). A new gang, the Atas Bats (fruit bats in pearls, led by Datin Ciku), and chapter eight, *Christmas Star* (jobs m46 to m55 and Hawker Tales k14 to k16), end at the Rain Vortex with a third ending. Every brand and landmark now has its real name (the MRT, Grab, Marina Bay Sands, Mustafa Centre and the rest); the people and the gangs stay invented. See **Orchard Road, Katong and Changi (twelfth pass)** below.

**Eleventh pass (16-11-2026): procedural contracts.** The story ends, the jobs don't: once the Phoenix Drum is home (job m25, the first ending) the phone's Contracts tab always holds three jobs made by a seeded generator from the job steps the game already has, given in person by the cast (each in their own Singlish voice) against the gang whose turf it is, with modifiers (a graveyard shift, a storm, a tight clock, no rides, the partner busy, FINE CITY wardens). A contract of the day is the same for every player that day, with your best time kept, and the rivals push back on your empire now and then, so there is always a district to retake. The city stays hand-painted; only the jobs are made. See **Procedural contracts**. Saves move to v12.

**Tenth pass (15-11-2026): the Chinatown empire.** A long goal that also works after the story: the eleven stalls and fifteen new properties (a shophouse, a zi char kitchen, a bumboat service, a karaoke lounge, a kopitiam, a taxi company, a provision shop, a rooftop bar, an ice kacang cart, a prata shop, a spice shophouse, a café, a textile house, a beach bar and a kayak hire) across all ten districts, each with three upgrades; nightly takings in the till, collected in person, at a safe house or by the partner; rival gangs raiding now and then (a text, a red pin and a clock: hold the shop, net them on the way in, or run down the one with the till); a missed raid costs the night's takings or leaves the place damaged until repaired, never lost; a hired jaga; six empire jobs; four ranks from Street Stall to Island Taipan; and the Empire tab on the phone and in the pause menu. See **The Chinatown empire**. Saves move to v11.

**Eighth pass (15-11-2026): guards, the partner and branches.** Guards, lookouts and wardens hear you and think: six states (patrol, suspicious, investigate, alert, search, back) with their own torch colours, bubbles, poses and sounds, calls for help and lures; the partner helps on their own (warns, fires at troublemakers, scoops loot, hides, waits at the getaway) with a three-way setting; holding a stall is a fight; clues leave painted traces at the spots they fit; and the disguise, trap and detective steps each offer two ways. See **Guards, the partner and branches**. Saves move to v9.

**Seventh pass (15-11-2026): job variety.** Most jobs had been chases. Seven new kinds of step (casing, the heist with a safe-dial mini-game, holding a stall, the disguise, the detective, traps and the crate swap) rebuild thirteen jobs around them, with the same givers and stories, and three new Hawker Tales show them off. A chase is now the climax of 9 of the 45 jobs (was 14 of 42); see **Job variety (seventh pass)** below.

**Sixth pass (15-11-2026): the island.** The city grows east into a fictional Singapore island: Chinatown, the river and the docks stay as they were, and two new districts open after the Lantern Festival, Toa Payoh (HDB slab and point blocks, void decks, a dragon playground, a neighbourhood centre, a wet market, a kopitiam, a hawker centre, a community club, a multi-storey car park and a longkang) and Marina Bay (glass towers on Raffles Place, Marina Bay Sands, the Float @ Marina Bay on the water, the Esplanade, the Singapore Flyer, Supertree Grove and a promenade), each with its own painted ground, buildings, props and townsfolk. Three expressways (the CTE, PIE and ECP, with crash barriers and gantries) join them. The MRT runs underground from Chinatown station to Raffles Place and Bayfront, then up onto a viaduct you can see, to Toa Payoh: fast travel with a painted ride view (a durian on the train is fined). Taxis come when you hail one with G or book one in the Grab app on the phone. Ticket wardens now also ride scooters. Chapter six, The Island, has exactly ten jobs: Director Kaw has turned the crows into a company, Kaypoh Crows Pte Ltd, and the Bukit Bunch, the Riverside Romp and a new gang, the Longkang Lizards, each have turf and a hideout on the island; there is a race against the train, a chase round the expressways and a finale on the bay front, then the island's ending. A notice queue stops hints, toasts and speech bubbles piling up. Wedding Cake Weather is easier on a first try (camera tickets no longer add heat, and one crash wobbles the cake once). Save v7.

**Fifth pass (15-11-2026): Singapore.** The quarter is now Singapore's Chinatown, named with real public street and place names (Smith Street, Temple Street, Pagoda Street, Trengganu Street, Keong Saik Road, Boat Quay, Clarke Quay, Tanjong Pagar) and no brands. There is a hawker centre whose five stalls are businesses, Uncle Teo's kopi counter with real kopi lingo and a glossary, choping tables with tissue packets, five-foot ways, an HDB void deck with Uncle Goh's chess table and a mama shop, a wet market, bumboats, Mr Ong the karang guni man, road-toll gantries, a "fine city" of ticket wardens, two new cheeky rival gangs (otters and macaques), thunderstorms, three festival nights and seven Hawker Tales side jobs. The magpies are now house crows, the Kaypoh Crows. Player 1 can use a gamepad, remap keys, turn on larger text and colour-blind-safe markers. Time trials, ghost races, race circuits and photo mode were dropped (owner, 15-11-2026).

## Rules and numbers

**Moving.** On foot: walk (sneak) 145, run 235, a dash at 560 for 0.22 s (0.75 s cooldown), and a hop of 0.42 s that clears low tiles (crates, hedges, benches). Controls are direct: the stick or keys set the heading and speed, with no drifting. Rides:

| Ride | Top speed | Hold | Moves on | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scooter | 340 | 8 | small (alleys too) | some are locked and need a jump-start |
| Trishaw | 250 | 16 | big | a seat for a fare |
| Tuk-tuk | 310 | 20 | big | a seat for a fare |
| Delivery van | 285 | 40 | big | too wide for alleys |
| Canal boat | 250 | 30 | water | the canal and harbour |

Boost (Space on a ride) and honk (Q) replace dash and hop. Four fire ladders lead to the rooftops; up there you cross gaps by hopping, nobody adds heat, and a hop that would land off a roof puts you back where you took off.

**Heat.** Up to five lanterns. Bumping a warden (dashing on foot, or riding over 130) adds 0.3, knocking a roadblock 0.25, hitting a car above 160 adds 0.15 (twice that on Normal, three times on Hard), and on Normal and Hard riding into a lit crossing against a red light adds 0.55 (Hard 0.8); on Assist these count half while a job is running. Wardens on bicycles come one per lantern (one fewer on Easy, at least one), up to four, from behind or to the side of the way you are heading; they ride at 205 on Easy or 240 on Normal plus 12 a lantern. From three lanterns roadblocks of fruit crates appear on the road ahead of a ride; from four a blimp follows where you were last seen, and its spotlight sees you within 120. Wardens see 520 with clear line of sight. Out of sight for 3.2 s (Easy) or 4.6 s (Normal) puts a lantern out, twice as fast in a hiding spot. A jacket swap at a safe house clears all heat. A warden who reaches you on foot (0.35 s on Easy, 0.2 s on Normal) or beside a stopped ride (1.1 s) catches you: a fine of 5% of your coins (10 to 150), a trip to the nearest safe house, and any job restarts from its checkpoint.

**Trading.** Base prices: jade bangles 120, porcelain bowls 58, silk fans 44, mooncakes 34, tea 22, mangoes 12. Each market leans 0.68 to 1.38 on each good (the Docks sell jade cheap, Clarke Quay pays most for it, Temple Hill sells tea cheap and Boat Quay pays for it, and so on), every night drifts each price by up to 14% either way, a contact's tip multiplies one good at one market, and the daily event adds one more. Buying costs 6% over the mid price and selling pays 6% under it, 3 points better for each reputation tier (20, 50 and 80). The haggle needle: in the gold middle the stall's prices improve by 14%, in the green by 7%, outside it nothing, and a sour miss costs 4%; the zone is wider with reputation and on Assist. You carry 4 on foot (plus one per cargo upgrade) or your ride's hold (plus a quarter of it per upgrade). Crows swoop every 25 to 45 s while you carry anything worth having (a chance of up to 50% by cargo value, 60% of that on Assist) and take two of your most valuable good unless you dash, boost, hop or hide as they reach you.

**Progress.** Each job pays coins and reputation in its district. Safe houses cost 150 to 300 and 15 reputation; each has a stash (60, plus 20 per cargo upgrade), the jacket swap, sleep till the next night (new prices), and the choice of hero. Six outfits per hero (120 to 400 coins and 20 to 30 reputation in a district), three garage upgrades with three levels (engine +7% top speed a level for 150, 400, 900; tyres +10% steering for 120, 350, 800; racks for 100, 300, 700) and five paints (free to 120) that apply to every ride. A night lasts 720 game minutes, one a second (a little slower on Assist). There are 20 trophies.

**Assist** (on by default, in Settings): a gold route line to the objective and the nearest job, gentler timers and lookouts, slower and rarer crows, half heat during jobs, a wider haggle zone, larger mini-game windows and a slower clock.

## Procedural contracts (eleventh pass)

The owner asked, once every job was done, whether the game could be procedural; the answer agreed was procedural jobs on the hand-painted city (not a procedural city). Desktop only; Assist on by default; no new paintings were needed (every contract is drawn with frames already in the atlases).

**The generator** (`lib/jade-proc.ts`) is a pure function of a reference, `{ seed, tier, stars, open districts }` (plus the giver and kind fixed when an offer was dealt, so a save rebuilds the same contract). It picks a giver (24 of the cast with a place of their own; Mr Ong rides his round, and a giver whose portrait is not painted yet gives no contracts), the gang that runs that district (the lizard and mynah bosses, Lepak and Bising, hire you against somebody else's gang), a kind, and up to two modifiers, then builds the steps on the city's real places and flow fields. Thirteen kinds, each composed of two to four existing step kinds:

| Kind | Steps |
|---|---|
| Delivery | pick up, (grab the bag), deliver against the clock (fragile, smelly or fined items) |
| Recovery | (tail), chase, pick up the loot, (lose the heat), back to the giver |
| Sting | lay nets on the gang's path, then chase the ringleader or tidy up |
| Undercover | tail a gang runner (in its own painted frames) to their store, swap the crates, (lose the heat) |
| Heist | case and crack a safe at one of the story's heist venues (quiet or loud) |
| Stand | hold a stall against waves of the gang, then chase the float or tidy up |
| Escort | walk the lion dance, then hold the stage or sweep up |
| Race | scooter (or on-foot) race along a route the flow field proves drivable, then pick up the fans' flyers |
| Detective | witnesses and spots with three-clue cases (one answer fits), then chase the thief |
| Disguise | the coat rack, the disguise past lookouts at a story venue, pick up and deliver |
| Safecracking | a lock, wires or the safe dial, then deliver |
| Clean-up | collect litter against the clock, then lose the warden |
| Turf | (a retake) trap or hold at the contested property, chase, pick up, report back |

Every place and spot is checked before a contract is accepted: in an open district, and reachable on foot from the giver (actors' routes, which cannot hop low props, follow the `walk` field). Twelve attempts with fresh sub-streams, then a plain errand round as the fallback (none in 1,200 test seeds). Modifiers only go where they fit: a tight clock on timed kinds (timers ×0.86), no rides not on races or heists (E on a ride is refused), partner busy not on team kinds (they wait at the giver; only your own F moves), FINE CITY (three fines on the delivery), the storm (rain forced on) and the graveyard shift (a darker tint; guards see 12% less far). The opening lines are the giver's own voice (`VOICE`), sometimes a gang boss's taunt, then the hero or partner on the modifiers; the closing line is the giver's thanks, and a fail toasts the giver's bark beside the failure card.

**Names.** Contract text never spells out a place, gang or landmark itself: places come from the city's own tables (`spotName`, `DISTRICT_NAME`), gangs from `GANG_NAME` in `lib/jade-props.ts`, people from `PEOPLE`, so a rename there (the owner has asked for real Singapore names; pass 12 does that sweep) flows through every contract. New text written for contracts uses real names (the MRT).

**Pay and difficulty.** Pay is (40 + each step's worth) × stars (1, 1.35, 1.75) × (1 + 0.15 × tier) × (1 + 0.1 a modifier), ×1.5 for the contract of the day and ×1.4 for a retake, rounded to 5; reputation 2 + stars. The tier (0 to 6) is your empire rank + half the districts where you own a property + one per eight contracts finished. The three offers are an easy, a middling and a hard slot (stars 1/1/2 at tier 0, 1/2/2 from tier 2, 1/2/3 from tier 3, 2/2/3 from tier 4). Without Assist a contract is a notch sharper than a story job (deliveries, clean-ups and the detective ×0.9 time, chases 4% faster, one more crow on an escort, the rival racer 3% quicker); with Assist the stand has one fewer per wave, chases are 7% slower and the swap 20% longer.

**The phone** (`lib/jade-contracts.ts`, `app/jade-street/contracts.tsx`). The Contracts tab (locked, with a note, until m25) shows the three offers, the contract of the day and any retake: the giver's face, title, district, kind, stars, modifier chips (hover for what they do), the brief and the pay, and Route me (pins it: the gold route line and its label lead to the giver). Offers are dealt fresh each night; a finished one is replaced at once. A contract's giver waves with a job pin and a coin over their head, and E beside them starts it (a story job by the same giver comes first). Between jobs, with no story job or Hawker Tale on offer, the gold line leads to the pinned contract's giver (else the nearest) and a plaque names the next contract. The job plaque reads *Contract*, *Contract of the day* or *Retake* with the modifiers.

**The contract of the day** is seeded by the date alone (`jade-cotd:<yyyy-mm-dd>`), always two stars at tier 3 in Chinatown, so every player gets the same one that day; it pays in full once a day (a quarter after that) and keeps your best time and runs for the day.

**The rivals push back.** Each new night after the first ending, if you own a property in an open district and nothing is contested, there is a 35% chance (30% on Assist) a gang muscles in on one of your districts: the keeper texts you, the district's tills take ×0.75 (×0.6 once they have dug in for three nights), and the Contracts tab offers a retake (a Turf contract at the contested property, ×1.4 pay). Winning it clears the contest and gives +4 reputation there. There is only ever one contest, so it never piles up.

**Saves** move to v12 (`jade-street-v12`; v11 saves get an empty contracts book, everything else kept). The jobs book's summary adds the contracts finished (kinds tried, retakes); `jobCounts` reads these from the save. Three trophies: Contractor (ten contracts), Job of the Day, Turf Holder (three retakes).

**Raids.** At 11 pm and 3 am (game clock 240 and 480) a gang may raid one of your properties with at least 30 coins in its till: 40% at each hour (30% on Assist), never during a job, one raid at a time. A fuller till draws them; a jaga halves a property's pull, and a damaged one is left alone. You get a text from the keeper, a toast, a red pin on the map and minimap, a pulsing red glow over the property with two gang members carrying off the till, and a clock on the HUD: 110 seconds (150 on Assist). The route line leads there. Arriving within 230 px (or E at the door) starts a raid job built for that property; the gang depends on the district (macaques in Chinatown and Temple Street, otters at Boat Quay and the docks, crows at Clarke Quay, lizards in Toa Payoh and Marina Bay, mynahs in Little India, Kampong Glam and Sentosa) and so does the kind of job:

- **Hold the shop** (the defend step): the gang comes at the counter in waves; dash into them.
- **Net them on the way in** (the trap step): three nets on the gang's way in, a 9-to-18-tile path walked tile by tile from a street corner 12 tiles out.
- **Run down the one with the till** (the chase step): a loop of four street corners round the property; bump the runner twice.
- **Catch the crows with the coins** (the crows' old grab-and-run raid job, Kaypoh Crows only).

Lizards never stand at a shop (trap or chase only). A win pays 30 coins plus 4% of the price, a point of reputation, and counts in the ledger. **A missed raid** (the clock runs out, or you give the job up) costs that night's takings from the till (half with a jaga). ## Guards, the partner and branches (eighth pass)

The owner's notes: guards should hear, wonder, look, chase, call for help and give up; the partner should think for themselves; holding a stall was too easy; the detective's clues should be shown, not only written; Office Hours was stiff on Hard; and more steps should branch than the heist.

**Hearing** (`lib/jade-aware.ts`). Dashes, hops, running footsteps (three a second above a walk, none while sneaking with Shift), crashes, knocked props, horns, gadgets landing and bursting, and the partner's distraction each make a noise with a radius: step 110, hop 170, hit 200, thud 230, dash 250, crash 330, distract 330, prop 360, confetti 420, horn 430, popper 470, the commotion 900. Under a five-foot way a noise carries half as far; hidden, 0.3 as far. Assist hears 0.75 as far, Hard 1.1. Thrown things, bursts and distractions are **lures**: a guard walks over to them even when you are nowhere near.

**Six states** per guard, lookout, gang watcher, trap gang member, Pip on a tail and foot warden, each with its own cone colour, bubble, pose and sound: **patrol** (the beat) → **suspicious** ("?", stops, turns toward it; a glimpse shorter than 0.6 of its reaction time is not enough) → **investigate** (walks to the spot it saw or heard, does not track you, looks round for 1.6 s, 2.2 s on Hard) → **alert** ("!", a shout, chases at 165 (140 Assist, 175 Hard) and calls every guard within 620 (460 Assist, 700 Hard) to the spot) → **search** (sweeps three spots round where it lost you for 6 s; 4 Assist, 7 Hard) → **back** (a grumble, walks home). Reaction time before acting: 0.6 s Normal, 0.75 Easy, 0.45 Hard, 1.05 Assist. A job fails when an alert guard keeps you in sight for 2 s (3 Assist, 1.6 Hard; not while it is still shouting) or reaches you. One shared `crewWatch` runs the case, heist and swap crews and the stealth lookouts; blend watchers, trap gangs, Pip and foot wardens use the same `mindStep`. Sixty guards cost about 0.06 ms a world step in Node.

**The partner on their own** (`lib/jade-pal.ts`; Settings → Partner: *Helps on their own* (default), *Only when I press F*, *Off*; with two players the partner is a person and none of this runs). F's tag-team moves are unchanged. On their own they:
- **warn** of a guard, lookout, watcher, camera or lizard 150 to 520 away (and wardens on foot when you ride or have heat) with a whisper, a pose and a pointing paw for 3 s; once per watcher, and no more than one every 6 s (25 s after a warden);
- **fire their own gadget** at raiders, gang members on the run with loot, swooping crows and pests chasing you, on a 4 s cooldown (3 s Assist); never at wardens, townsfolk, job guards, lookouts, witnesses or the crowd (`palFair`), and their shots pass through them;
- **scoop dropped loot** within 260 of them and hand it over;
- **curl up** (or peek out) where they are when you hide with a guard within 420, instead of crossing its torch;
- **wait at the getaway scooter** on a loud heist, beckoning;
- **tidy the stall** between waves (+0.1 once a calm spell) and stand guard on it.
Off means they wait during jobs (a defend job leaves the stall to you) but F still sends them where a job needs two.

**Holding a stall** is a fight now: raiders come in trios from three sides 600 away (the queue is `per + 2 × wave + 1`), every third from wave 2 (two in three from wave 3, off Assist) is a **sneak** under a box from the side away from you, slower (×0.72) and only bowled over by a dash, hop or ride; the partner on guard does not see sneaks. A raider at the stand stays 1.6 s (2.2 Assist) chewing at it (0.16 a second, 0.03 Assist, ×1.2 Hard) then bites (0.21, 0.05 Assist) and runs. A gadget knocks a sneak's box off, and off Assist leaves a raider dizzy for 1.7 s instead of shooing it. The stall's painting cracks in three stages as it loses health.

**Clue markers.** Each search spot is painted as what it is (curry puff crates, blue bins, a cooler box, planters, a bicycle rack, pandan baskets, a newspaper bundle, kueh steamers, cake boxes, a bin, an awning, a kopi stack). Each clue you hold paints its trace in front of every spot it fits: bird prints (under a roof), crumbs (food), a grey feather (blue), pandan syrup (sweet), claw scratches (high), paper scraps (paper). The spot with every trace is the one.

**Branch cards.** Three more step kinds ask which way, on two painted cards (1 and 2, or a click), like the heist:

| Step | First way | Second way |
|---|---|---|
| blend | **Disguise**: the coat from the rack, walk with the crowd | **Commotion**: the partner bangs a wok lid; every lookout stares at them for 9 s (13 Assist); go. No disguise: after the racket a lookout who sees you fills Suspicion fast |
| trap | **Nets**: lay nets and bait, spring them with F | **Chase**: no nets; dash into each gang member before it reaches the end of its path; one that sees you bolts at ×1.55 |
| detective | **Ask around**: pick the right question of three | **Kopi bribe**: E hands a witness a kopi for 15 coins and they tell you straight away; ask normally when the coins run out |

## Art pipeline

Codex paints every sprite, building, ride, person, prop, ground texture, mini-game scene, portrait and interface piece; code draws only light, glow, shadow, fog, lantern cables and route lines. The prompts are in `docs/jade-street-art/jobs.py` and `jobs2.py` (the second batch: the loading screen, five stall scenes with their keepers, the map paper, frame, icons and fog, the storybook, chapter cards and ending, medals, phone and controls pictures, new hero actions, seated townsfolk, street events, street props, the festival, fireworks and 24 buildings and landmarks) with the shared style in `style.txt`, and `gen.py` runs Codex image generation (up to six at once). The painted mock-ups that set the look are `mock-play.jpg`, `mock-city.jpg` and `mock-trade.jpg` there. `python3 scripts/jade-art.py <folder of paintings>` keys out the flat background, removes the magenta cast from fur and jackets, keeps Dora's fur cream-white, cuts each sheet into frames, scales them to world size, packs nine WebP atlases (about 13 MB with the textures and the full-screen paintings, in `public/art/jade-street/`) and writes `lib/jade-art.ts`. Each hero has walk and run cycles in three facings, idle, sneak, dash, hop, carry, bump, pickup, hide, climb, talk, swap, paint, pick, door, sleep, wave, cheer, caught, and riding poses for the scooter (and a bump and a cheer on it), the trishaw's pedals and the boat's pole; in a tuk-tuk or van the hero is inside the cab. Outfits and paints are hue shifts of the painted frames, cached per variant. The fifteenth pass's four rides and both heroes' poses on them (prompts in `jobs15.py`) are packed by `python3 scripts/jade-art15.py <folder>` into their own atlas, `atlas-rides15.webp` (core tier until the sixteenth pass, now the job tier), appended to `lib/jade-art.ts` without repacking the rest.

The sixth pass's art is in `docs/jade-street-art/jobs6.py`: thirteen sheets (two each of HDB and bay buildings, two of island props, the rides (MRT cars, the viaduct, two taxis, the scooter warden), the lizards, the island's portraits, three townsfolk, the island's characters doing things, the heroes' new actions, and the island's interface icons), six ground textures (expressway, HDB tiles, playground rubber, granite, boardwalk, bay water) and five backdrops (the chapter-six card, the island ending, the MRT ride, and the two new stall scenes). The packer puts them in `atlas-isle` (2048 × 1802, about 980 KB) and `atlas-isle2` (2048 × 1119, about 630 KB). The job givers are `docs/jade-street-art/jobs7.py`: seven sheets (four givers a sheet, six frames each, and Tock on his own), each painted with a strip of the givers' portraits as the reference so the bodies match the faces, packed into `atlas-cast` (2048 × 988, about 930 KB). All painted art for the game is now about 23.5 MB (19.1 MB after the fifth pass), all decoded behind the loading screen.

## Loading and memory (16-11-2026)

The paintings come to about 25 MB to download and about 360 MB once decoded, which took minutes to load on a home connection or in Safari, and the whole lot sat decoded for the whole session. Now they load by need:

- **Tiers.** `lib/jade-load.ts` gives every atlas and ground texture a tier. `core` is the heroes' everyday poses (`atlas-dora`, `atlas-enzo`: walking, running, riding, firing, talking and so on), the interface, the townsfolk with every neutral portrait and the heroes' moods, the traffic, and Chinatown's ground. `town` is Chinatown's buildings, street props, stalls and properties at level 0, the Singapore street pieces and the givers who live there. Those two are the eager set: the only thing the loading bar counts. `job` (the heroes' rarer poses in `atlas-dora2`/`atlas-enzo2`, Chinatown's dialogue faces, set pieces, job props, gadgets, guards, the gangs, the upgraded stalls and properties, the festival and fireworks) streams in as soon as the eager set is in, even on the title. `mini` (`atlas-mg`) loads when a mini-game opens. Each later zone (`isle`: Toa Payoh and Marina Bay; `east`: Little India and Kampong Glam; `resort`: Sentosa; `far`: Orchard Road, Katong and Changi) has its own atlases, including its givers (`atlas-cast-<zone>`) and dialogue faces (`atlas-faces-<zone>`).
- **The title opens at once.** The page starts loading the eager set when it mounts and shows the title straight away; New game or Continue before the eager set is in shows the painted loading screen (lanterns, Try again on a failure) until it is.
- **The world waits for what it needs.** Each quarter second the controller (`Play.artTick` in `app/jade-street/controller.ts`) works out the zone each hero stands in and the zones within 34 tiles of either. What the spot needs (`needFor`: the core, the heroes' zones, the job tier while a job runs, Chinatown's faces while someone other than a hero talks, the mini-game pieces while one is open) must be decoded before the world steps: until then the world holds and, after a quarter second, the same painted lanterns cover the street (`ArtWait`, `data-testid="jade-art-wait"`). The zones near the heroes stream in behind, two files at a time; the zones the save has opened are fetched into the browser's cache without decoding, so an MRT ride or a taxi finds them on disk.
- **Drawing never waits.** `src()` in `lib/jade-scene.ts` returns nothing for a frame whose atlas is not in (the caller skips it; `drawHero` falls back to the idle frame, so a hero is never blank), notes the atlas in `artMissing` (the controller loads it at once: a giver waiting outside their own district, say) and marks the ground chunk being painted, which is repainted in place when any painting arrives (`artGen`). The minimap and the painted map paint the whole island, so their misses are not asked for (`quietMiss`); they repaint at most every few seconds while pictures are still coming in.
- **Letting go.** Each painting is fetched and decoded into an `ImageBitmap` (an image element where the browser has no `createImageBitmap`). Every two seconds the controller lets go (`toRelease` in `lib/jade-load.ts`) of zone and mini-game files nothing has drawn from for 45 seconds that are not wanted now, and, while the decoded paintings still come to more than `ART_BUDGET` (250 MB; core and Chinatown are 135 MB, the job tier 100 MB, everything 360 MB), the longest-idle others outside the heroes' own zone. A file let go is closed, so its pixels are freed at once rather than whenever the browser gets round to it, and the recoloured frames cut from it go too. The background queue only fetches (does not decode) a nearby zone that would take the paintings past the budget. A file let go that is drawn again is asked for again at once, so letting go of something still wanted costs a moment's reload, never a blank hero.
- **The glow leak (the likely crash).** `glowSprite` caches a 64 px gradient canvas per colour string, and the fading glows (sky lanterns, firework bursts, set-piece warnings, the empire raid, the paw marker) put their alpha into the string, so every frame made new canvases that were never freed: about 75 a second while playing, 11,000 DOM nodes and almost 500 MB in two and a half minutes of standing still in Chromium, and it was there before the loading work. `Scene.glow` now splits the alpha off (`splitAlpha`) and stamps one sprite per colour at `globalAlpha` (the same pixels, within 1/255), and the cache keeps its 64 last used colours and empties the rest. The memory soak fails if the glows pass 64 or the DOM nodes keep piling up.
- **Canvases.** The ground chunks are capped at 44 and about 96 MB of pixels (`CHUNK_PX`; a Retina screen's chunks are up to 820 px square), and an evicted chunk's canvas is emptied at once. Recoloured frames (outfits and paint jobs) are capped at 360, least recently drawn first, and the classic jacket or an unpainted ride is drawn straight from the atlas instead of a copy. Every off-screen canvas goes through `ctx2d()`, which returns null instead of throwing when WebKit refuses a context past its canvas memory cap; the caller then skips that cache.
- **Smaller files.** `scripts/jade-art.py` saves the atlases at WebP quality 80 (was 86), about 15% smaller with no visible difference at the game's scale, and splits the old `atlas-faces`, `atlas-cast`, `atlas-props`, `atlas-set`, `atlas-sg`, `atlas-empire`, `atlas-east2` and both hero atlases by tier (45 atlases, was 24).
- **Caching.** The packer writes a content hash of every file in `public/art/jade-street/` into `VER` in `lib/jade-art.ts` and stamps it into the stylesheets' URLs; the game asks for `name.webp?v=<hash>` (`artUrl` in `app/jade-street/ui.tsx` and `lib/jade-scene.ts`), and `public/_headers` tells Cloudflare to serve `/art/jade-street/*` with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable`. Workers static assets ignore the query when matching the file, and vinext only writes its own `_headers` (for `/_next/static/*`) when `public/` has none, so that rule is repeated in ours. `tests/jade-load.mjs` fails when a hash is stale.
- **Tests.** The e2e suite checks the title, the early-start loader and Try again first, then calls `__jade.artAll()` (decode everything, let nothing go) so its teleports never hold the world mid-check. `tests/jade-load.mjs` covers the tiers and the need, stream and release rules, `tests/e2e/jade-street-load.mjs` times the title and Chinatown under throttling, and `tests/e2e/jade-street-memory.mjs` is the ten-minute soak.
- **Measured (16-11-2026, a local production build under `wrangler dev`, 40 ms latency).** `tests/e2e/jade-street-load.mjs` in Chromium: at 10 Mbps the title opened in 19.2 s before and 1.1 s now, and Chinatown was playable in 19.3 s before and 8.3 s now (9.3 MB); at 3 Mbps the title took 63.6 s before and 2.5 s now, playable 26.4 s now; a repeat visit opens the title in 0.2 s and is playable in 0.4 s (was 1.4 s and 1.6 s, every painting revalidated). WebKit, unthrottled: title 1.1 s to 0.5 s, playable 2.7 s to 1.7 s. `tests/e2e/jade-street-memory.mjs`, ten minutes hopping across all thirteen districts, before and after run back to back on 17-11-2026: the browser's resident memory averaged 2,145 MB before and 1,261 MB now in Chromium (peak 2,679 and 1,316), and 2,030 MB before and 973 MB now in WebKit (peak 2,391 and 1,063). Before, it climbed all run (the glow leak); now it levels off within two minutes. The decoded paintings stay around 250 MB (were 360 MB from the start), the glow sprites at 25 at most, and the DOM at about 300 nodes (11,000 after two and a half minutes before).

## What is weakest

- **Chapter nine is new places and people, not new mechanics.** Every step is a kind the story already had, the otters, lizards and crows return as Towkay Hoot's hired help (there is no new gang; the stage is raided by crows because otters proved far too hard to hold a stall against: the Normal bot won 2 of 8), and The Lantern Lions is the lion-dance escort of chapter two on a new route.
- **The coast's properties still reuse paintings.** The sixteenth pass painted the south shore's six stands; the six Katong, Orchard and Changi properties of the twelfth pass still share the tenth pass's stands of the same trade.
- **The south shore is an island of its own.** Tiong Bahru, Clarke Quay and Jurong sit side by side south of Kampong Glam over two long bridges, nothing like their real places on the map; the walk over a bridge is long (about 14 tiles of open water), so the MRT is the way in.
- **The river and the stream have square banks.** Jurong Lake has a painted bank laid over its stepped tiles (the sixteenth pass); the Singapore River is still a straight four-tile channel and the stream into the lake is whole tiles.
- **The Slingshot is a lift on the spot.** The rider and the capsule go straight up and come down; there is no view from the top.
- **Clarke Quay's stage is as near Tiong Bahru station as its own.** The station is on the south bank (as the real one is), so a pin on the north bank can plan a ride to either.
- **Katong's ground is plain cream tiles now**, calm at full zoom; it is a single texture, so the whole district is the same floor.
- **The engine tests are slow.** `npm run test:jade` plays every job again after a forced failure at every step; with 75 jobs it takes about 30 minutes on this Mac (with other work running), and the full bot run about an hour.
- **The decoded paintings peak nearer the soak's limit.** 310 to 318 MB against a 320 MB limit when the tour hops all sixteen districts every 20 seconds (314 MB after the sixteenth pass, which moved the fifteenth pass's ride atlas out of the core to make room). Ordinary play stays near the 250 MB budget, and the next painted atlas needs room made for it first.

- **Route me's chains rarely fire.** It weighs two rides back to back (the sixteenth pass), but the MRT reaches Sentosa with a flat ride time, so on today's map no trip between two places is clearly quicker by two rides; the ride's gates and view still count as a fixed few seconds.
- **One contract offer is still a heavy frame.** A new night's three offers are now dealt one a frame, but each still builds its route's flow fields over the whole city: one frame of about 8 to 25 ms per offer, three times a night.
- **The ride jobs are short.** Two Scoops is a casing step with a lure, Picnic Post is a delivery with F, Bak Chang by Boat is a 17-second paddle, and Down Siloso Road is an ordinary chase on a fast cart. They show the rides off rather than build new mechanics.
- **Frame rate is above the floor on average, not every moment.** Every district averages 56.8 or better, but busy half-seconds dip to 42 to 46 (rain, a crowd and a fresh street all at once).
- **The calendar is only as right as the device.** Festival dates are tables for 2025 to 2032; after 2032 Deepavali and Hari Raya fall back to no festival, and a wrong device clock gives the wrong festival.

- **Contracts are compositions, not new mechanics.** Every contract is built from the step kinds the story already uses, on the story's heist and disguise venues, so a player who has done the story has seen every piece; the variety is in the mix, the place, the giver, the gang and the modifiers.
- **Races are the contracts most often failed first time**, and only the timer kinds get a Tight clock; stings and the detective are rarer than the others because their places are harder to fit.
- **A giver needs paintings to give contracts.** The generator skips anyone without a painted face and body (`npc-<id>-idle-0`); every one of the 24 givers has both today, so this only matters for people added later.
- **The contract of the day uses the device's date**, so players in different time zones roll over at their own midnight, and nothing stops a player changing the clock.
- **The graveyard shift is a tint.** It darkens the screen and shortens guards' sight; the lamps and windows are not relit for it.

- **Empire jobs are generated, not authored.** The supplier deal, inspector, price war, fares, tours and karaoke night build their routes from the property's spot (litter rings, downhill tail routes), so a few play out in plain streets and the same job at two properties feels alike.
- **A smashed shop's till.** A damaged property's till cap once fell to one coin, so damage quietly emptied the till as well (the cause of an e2e check that failed one run in three). The till now never shrinks on its own; the fix landed after the full bot run, which it cannot make harder.

- **Frame rate was measured on a busy machine.** `play:jade` fails its 55 fps floor on some runs because of other work on the Mac (market 54.3 to 59.1 and guards 36.8 to 60.0 across runs of the same code); the ninth pass has not been timed on a quiet machine.
- **The swap's wait spot is a fixed 170 units past the crate.** When every crate is watched, the chevron points at a spot away from the guard, not at true cover.
- **A job interrupted by closing the game restarts from the beginning.** The reload says which job stopped and routes to its giver, but does not resume from the checkpoint.

- **Sound goes through walls.** A noise is a circle: a guard behind a container hears a dash as well as one in the open. Only a five-foot way (half) and hiding (0.3) muffle it.
- **Guards call only their own crew.** An alert job guard brings the other guards in that job; street wardens and gang members never answer it, and wardens on foot only hear and look (they do not search).
- **The partner on their own is simple.** They warn of each watcher once per job (no more than one warning every 6 s), fire at the nearest fair target without leading it, and only when they carry a gadget with ammo; they never pick a tag-team move for you. *Off* means waiting during jobs (a stall is left to you), not leaving.
- **The clue markers make the detective easier.** With the traces drawn, the right spot is the one with every mark, so the puzzle is now mostly in asking (or paying); the bribe removes even that for 15 coins a witness.
- **The branches are two ways, not three.** The commotion, the chase and the bribe change how a step plays, but each is still one set-up in one place, and the witnesses still always tell the truth.
- **The stall's difficulty comes from numbers.** Sneaks are told apart only by their box or branch; the partner on guard cannot see them, which some players may find unfair rather than sneaky.
- **Office Hours is now easy on Normal** (8 of 8 first time, was 5): the guards that need a real glimpse and walk to a noise rather than track you made both levels fairer. Hard is 7 of 8.
- **The MRT station sign is not a new painting.** The station markers reuse the painted train pin and station lamp already in the atlas; a dedicated roundel sheet was tried, but the image tool was at its usage limit. (The hint's 20 s now only count while it can show, so a banner or a trophy toast no longer eats into it.)
- **Seven of the 45 jobs still end in a chase** (and two in the loot-bag grab): the heirloom chases and the Pounce tutorial stay, by design.

- **The island is a compression, not a map.** Toa Payoh and Marina Bay sit side by side across a few expressways (about 30 seconds' ride from Chinatown by scooter); the real places are kilometres apart. The names are real and the parody landmarks stand roughly where their originals would, but the layout is invented, and Chinatown's own grid is still the fifth pass's.
- **The trains are watched, not boarded.** The MRT's trains run overhead on a real timetable, but riding is fast travel from the gates with a painted ride view; you never stand on a platform or in a carriage in the world. The pole and seat poses were painted for the carriage but the ride view's painting already shows both heroes, so they are not used.
- **Taxis drive themselves through traffic.** The autopilot follows the big rides' flow field and passes through cars rather than queueing; it is a ride, not a driving simulation.
- **Expressways are fast for traffic, not for you.** Cars cruise about twice as fast on them, but your scooter or van keeps its usual top speed; they are quick because they are straight and have no lights.
- **Scooter wardens are free-roam only.** New ones do not appear during a job (they made deliveries and Hot Goods too punishing), so the five-foot-way fine and their wider sight are felt mostly between jobs.
- **The givers stand still.** They breathe, wave and talk, but they never walk (only front-facing frames were painted), always face the camera rather than you, and a visiting giver simply appears beside the spot. Ms Vanda's place is on the Float @ Marina Bay, so she shares it with the stage dance: E offers whichever is nearer.
- **Chinatown is a little slower than it was.** Under the test load the Chinatown districts average 52 to 56 fps in headless Chromium against 56 to 58 for the fifth pass on the same machine the same hour; the island districts hold 56 to 58. The draw itself costs about the same, so the cause is still open.
- **Chapter six is stiffer on Hard.** 76% first time against 85% for the older jobs; easing it further made Normal too easy.
- **Loading is heavier.** Two more atlases and eleven new textures and backdrops add about 3.5 MB, all decoded before play starts.
- **Chapter six reuses the job kit.** The ten jobs are new routes, people and set-ups, and two new step kinds (ride the train, race the train), but most of their steps (chases, tails, escorts, collecting, stealth, fares) play like the earlier chapters'.

- **Singapore is dressed onto the old plan.** The streets carry real names and the new places are painted in, but the layout under them is still the invented quarter's grid; it is not a map of Chinatown. The full island (pass 6) is where that changes.
- **The fine city is felt mostly on foot.** Cameras catch red lights and speeding on a ride, but ticket wardens walk, so a player who rides everywhere meets fewer of them; jaywalking needs a warden in sight.
- **Singlish is light by design.** About forty short barks and the kopi lingo; townsfolk do not hold conversations, and nobody speaks in recorded voice.
- **The gangs are street events, not crews.** Each is a short chase-and-recover event with its own gadget rules and one side job; they have no hideout or story arc.
- **The gamepad was tested with a fake pad** in the browser suite (stick, buttons, menus and mini-games), not with a real controller; there is no on-screen button legend for the pad beyond the Controls page and the remap panel's note.
- **Roads are still straight.** Bending them would have meant rechecking traffic lanes, lights, gantries and every job's route, so it was left out.

- **The partner is guided, not clever.** They follow on the flow fields and catch up out of sight when stuck or far off screen; they never pick a fight, fire a gadget or choose a tag-team move on their own. That is on purpose (direct control and predictability first), but it means the partner only helps when you press F.
- **Two players share one screen.** Player 2 has the right side of the keyboard or a gamepad; player 1 now has a gamepad too (fifth pass). The gamepad was tested with a fake pad in the browser suite, not a real controller.
- **Thirteen team jobs, not twenty-five.** The other twelve still play as before with the partner tagging along (and cheering), which is fine for deliveries and races but is a missed chance in a few.
- **Gadgets make some jobs easier.** A net on a getaway or paint on a lookout is quicker than doing it the old way; the medals' par times were not retuned for gadgets, so golds are a little easier than on the third pass.

- **Normal's failures are not spread across most jobs.** First tries fail in ten of the 25 jobs on Normal; the other fifteen (deliveries with generous timers, the tutorial jobs, side trips) are hard to fail for a steady player. Tightening them further made the easy jobs unfair for the casual bot before it made them interesting.
- **Hard leans on two jobs.** Container Maze and Hot Goods account for most of Hard's retries; Container Maze averages 3.6 tries, a little over the three we aimed for, even with the retry easing.
- **The bots are not people.** They react late, wobble and lapse, and they now wait at red lights, but they do not misread a telegraph or panic; the numbers are a guide, not a measurement of players.
- **The set pieces are scripted to each chase's route.** They fire where the thief goes, so a player who cuts across the city can sometimes miss one.
- **The businesses were a modest economy** (now the empire, tenth pass). Six stalls at their grandest earn about 1,745 coins a night against 23,550 to buy everything; there is nothing to spend a fortune on once the outfits and garage are bought.
- **The city is still built on a grid underneath.** Landmarks, plazas and curving paths break it up, but the roads themselves are straight.
- **Phones are not a target.** The game is desktop-only; the older touch checks still pass in Chromium's phone emulation, but the third pass's features were not designed or checked for phones.
- **The contrast check skips text over paintings or the canvas**, where no single backing colour exists; those labels rely on their dark plates and outlines.
- **Sound is synthesized**: the score, babble voices, radio, ambience and effects are WebAudio; there are no recorded instruments or voices.
